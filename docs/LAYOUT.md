# Matching Word's layout

How the editor lays out pages so they match Word's, how that is measured, and the rules found along the way. Code
comments say *why* a rule exists; this file says what the rules are and how to check a change against Word.

## How it's measured

- **Reference:** Word's own PDFs of a set of real documents (151 private documents kept outside the repository, plus
  a 19-document corpus generated by `test/fixtures/make-layout-corpus*.mjs`). Word prints each one (read-only, through
  COM); the editor prints the same file through Edge.
- **Comparison:** the text lines of both PDFs are extracted, matched in order, and compared on the same page. A line
  counts as matching when its baseline is within 1px of Word's. Reported: page counts, lines on the same page, lines
  within 1px, and "steps" (the distance between consecutive lines, within 1px).
- **Where it stands:** the 151 documents: every page count right, 99.0% of lines within 1px. The corpus: 97.7%.
- **Rule for changes:** a change to layout or print is kept only when the full comparison doesn't get worse overall,
  and its comment says what it fixed. Fitted constants (a font's print offset) say which measurement they came from.
- The tools (print with Word, print with Edge, extract lines, compare) live in `tools/word-compare`; see
  CONTRIBUTING.md for running them.

## How layout works

1. **Paragraph and character formatting** become CSS on the document's paragraphs and runs (`ooxml/props.js`,
   `ooxml/styles.js`, the paragraph pass in `editor/editor.js`). Line heights use each font's own metrics
   (`engine/metrics.json`, from the installed fonts' hhea tables), not the browser's `normal`.
   **A line is as tall as the text on it** (`editor/textspans.js`), as Word sizes it: every text node is in a span of
   its own carrying the line height (`--odx-lhv`, a registered property), and the paragraph and the formatting around
   the text have line-height 0. A browser in standards mode otherwise counts each paragraph's own font on every line (its
   strut), making lines of smaller runs, picture lines and empty lines taller. In quirks mode it leaves out boxes
   without text of their own; the rules here reproduce that, so the layout is the same in both modes (99.0% within 1px
   either way). A line holding only a line break keeps the paragraph's height, as in Word. Set line heights in document
   content with `--odx-lhv`, not `line-height`, and read a paragraph's with `lineHeightOf` (`editor/util.js`).
2. **Line pitch** (`editor/lhfix.js`): Edge rounds a font's ascent and descent to whole pixels, so lines come out a
   little shorter than Word's. Each paragraph style gets the line height whose laid-out pitch matches Word's.
3. **Widths** (`editor/widths.js`): fonts Edge draws with hinted advances get the difference back as letter spacing,
   so lines break where Word's do. Justified lines squeeze their spaces as Word does (`editor/justify.js`).
4. **Pagination** (`editor/pages.js`): the editor is one continuous column; paragraphs split line by line and tables
   row by row (rows inside their cells) at each page's bottom, with Word's widow/orphan, keep-with-next and
   keep-lines rules. Spacers and margins are view decorations, never document content. Sections, columns, footnotes,
   headers and footers, floating pictures and tables are placed here too.
5. **Print** (`engine/printlines.js`, `editor/baseline.js`): Edge prints every block and line at a whole pixel. The
   print layer draws each line as its own element moved to its exact position, plus a baseline shift per font that
   puts the text where Word puts it. Pages are a whole pixel apart, as Edge prints them.

## Rules learned

**Lines and spacing**
- A line's extra "multiple" spacing goes below its text; "at least" spacing puts its extra above.
- An exactly spaced line must end a little above the bottom margin to stay on its page.
- Contextual spacing removes only a paragraph's own space.
- A paragraph mark's size sets the last line's height only when the paragraph shows no text; an empty list item has
  a line.
- A run of spaces beside text doesn't change a paragraph's uniform font and size.
- Superscript and subscript don't make their line taller.
- A symbol bullet makes only its own line taller, by its ascent over the text's descent; wrapped lines keep the
  text's height.
- Text in the paragraph's own font with the taller ascent sets the line's baseline.

- Emphasis marks make their line taller by about 0.31em: above it, or below for marks under the text.
- A border round a run makes its line taller by what the border and its space reach past half a point, above and below.
- Right-to-left paragraphs: their left and right (alignment, indents) are their start and end; tab stops count from
  the right margin.

**Pages**
- A section's vertical alignment moves its text on each of its pages: centred between the margins, at the bottom,
  or with the room left shared out between its paragraphs (both). The empty paragraph that only ends a section takes
  no room there, and has no line number.
- Line numbers sit 0.25in left of the text (or w:distance), right-aligned on each line's baseline; they count from
  w:start + 1, and "continuous" goes on from the section before.
- A paragraph taller than a page that can't split where it starts moves to the next page and splits there.
- A table row moves whole when a cell that breaks would keep none of its lines, or when a row with a minimum height
  doesn't fit that height.
- A line carried to the next page goes over with its line box, not its text box, at the page's top.
- A page break followed by an empty next-page section break makes one new page, not two.
- A section's first paragraph loses the space after of the paragraph ending the section before.
- Even and odd headers go by the page's number, not its place.

**Pictures, shapes and tables**
- Pictures are sized to a hundredth of a pixel, not whole pixels.
- An inline picture's effect extent adds room around it.
- A column-wide wrapped picture takes a band of its own; floats inside the band overlap it.
- A floating table longer than a page runs on in the flow.
- A text watermark is its text fitted to its shape (the advance across, the font's line down), turned as it says and
  centred on the margins; legal numbering (isLgl) shows every level in Arabic numerals, its own included.
- A line between two table rows belongs to the lower row; a row's cells share its top and bottom margins.

**Fonts**
- A font the computer lacks is set in the stand-in its font table names (w:altName), else a known substitute
  (DejaVu Sans in Verdana, Liberation fonts in their Microsoft equivalents).
- Edge shapes text at its font size cut to 1/100px; the missing share comes back as letter spacing.
- Kerning is off unless the document turns it on (w:kern).

**Print**
- Each font has a fixed print baseline offset (a pixel amount, not a share of the em), fitted on Word's PDFs.
- Sections with different page sizes print each page as its own named @page.

**Fields** (Word's PDFs of `test/fixtures/fields.docx`, as saved and with its fields updated)
- A running head (STYLEREF) is the first paragraph in its style on the page; on a page with none, the last before it;
  before the first, the first after it. With \l, the last on the page.
- PAGEREF with \p is "above" or "below" on the same page, else "on page N"; a page's number is in its section's
  numbering and format.
- Word updates the body's fields as it prints or exports a PDF (an unused style's STYLEREF becomes "Error! No text
  of specified style in document.", a missing style's "Error! Use the Home tab..."); on screen it keeps their saved
  results until F9. A STYLEREF naming a character style finds text in that style (sample S106's footer). (Sample
  S106's stored Word PDF is older and shows a body STYLEREF's saved text; Word's export today updates it.)

**Lists, fit text and defaults** (Word's PDFs of `test/fixtures/lists-more.docx`, `fittext.docx`, and a file with no
styles part)
- A list number takes the bold, italic, colour and underline of its level's w:rPr, else of the paragraph mark.
- Number formats Word knows by name (ordinal, cardinalText, circled numbers, Arabic and Russian letters...) give the
  labels in `test/fixtures/lists-more.word-labels.json`; the Chinese stems and branches go on as digits past 10 and 12.
- Fit text: the text spans exactly its w:val, the difference shared out after each character but the last; runs with
  the same w:id are one group.
- A paragraph bar border (w:pBdr/w:bar) isn't drawn by Word: not by us either.
- With no styles part, Word's text is Aptos 12pt, 8pt after, 1.15 lines.
- A field's result inside another field's code (a nested PAGE in an IF) isn't shown.

**Compatibility rules** (settings.xml w:compat, which older documents carry; each checked with Word's PDFs of a test
document in Word 2003's mode, with the rule and without it: `test/fixtures/mkcompat.mjs`)
- `splitPgBreakAndParaMark`: the paragraph mark after a page break goes to the next page, an empty line at its top.
- `doNotUseIndentAsNumberingTabStop`: a hanging indent is a list label's tab stop only when it comes before the next
  tab stop.
- `doNotUseHTMLParagraphAutoSpacing`: automatic spacing is a plain 5pt each side, added to its neighbour's and kept at
  the top of a page. Without it, it is 14pt, shared with the paragraph beside it, none at the top of a page or a cell,
  and none between two items of one list.
- `useFELayout`, `doNotBreakWrappedTables`, `forgetLastTabAlignment`, `underlineTabInNumList`: Word draws the test
  documents the same with them and without them.

## Tried and dropped

- Lifting text by half the leading (hurt documents with exact spacing).
- A flat negative word spacing on justified paragraphs: Word's squeeze is per line.
- Narrower lines (0.5px less room) to match Word's wrapping: broke far more lines than it fixed.
- Per-run line heights for every font (kept to tall fonts only).
- Shifting every line of a page that starts mid-paragraph by half its leading.
- Size-specific print offsets on top of each font's offset.
- One print copy per run of same-size pages (Edge printed the run's pages over one another).
- Cambria Math line boxes for symbols the text's font lacks.

## Open differences

- Text after a floating table that leaves a narrow gap beside it (under an inch): Word sets the text in that gap.
- A line holding superscript and subscript can break a word earlier than Word's.

- A full-width anchored picture whose following text Word places about 56px lower than the anchor's empty
  paragraphs explain.
- Lines whose wrap differs from Word's by less than a hundredth of a pixel.
- Very long documents with mixed page sizes print slowly (a copy of the sheet per page).
